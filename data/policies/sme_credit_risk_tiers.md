# SME Credit Risk Tier Policy

Policy owner: Credit Risk Management. Applies to all lending to small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) customers.

## 1. Purpose

Every SME loan is assigned a risk tier from A (lowest risk) to D (highest risk). The tier drives pricing, approval authority and monitoring intensity. Exposure in this policy means the sum of the outstanding balances of the loans concerned.

## 2. Tier Definitions

1. Tier A: strong financials, no arrears in the last 12 months, debt service coverage ratio of at least 1.5.
2. Tier B: sound financials, no arrears of more than 30 days in the last 12 months, coverage ratio of at least 1.2.
3. Tier C: weakened financials, or currently between 31 and 90 days past due.
4. Tier D: more than 90 days past due, in default, or under restructuring. Every loan more than 90 days past due must be in tier D.

## 3. Past-Due Monitoring

1. A loan that is more than 30 days past due is placed on the watchlist and reviewed by the RM every month.
2. A loan that is more than 60 days past due requires a written recovery plan within 15 business days.
3. A loan that is more than 90 days past due is classified as defaulted and transferred to the Workout team.
4. The RM must report the total exposure of past-due loans in their portfolio to Credit Risk Management at the end of each month.

## 4. Approval Authority

1. An RM may approve new SME lending up to 150,000 EUR for tier A and B customers.
2. New lending to tier C customers, and any single-customer exposure above 400,000 EUR, requires credit committee approval.
3. No new lending is granted to tier D customers.

## 5. Concentration Limits

1. Tier D exposure may not exceed 8% of an RM's total SME portfolio exposure. If it does, the RM's portfolio is reviewed by Credit Risk Management.
2. Exposure to a single industry sector may not exceed 25% of the bank's SME portfolio.
3. Tiers are reassessed at least every 12 months, and immediately after any loan becomes more than 30 days past due.
